- **Key numbers** and **How it's calculated** are your cheat sheet if you are asked where a figure comes from.
- **If they ask** has short, ready answers for likely questions; the appendix slides are backups to show only when asked.

## Slide 2 — Problem

### Say

*Time: ~23s*

This is one power bank — the Anker 25,000, 165 watt. Same model. On one app's search page, it's listed at 100,000 dinars… [click] and right beside it, at 200,000. [click] Across Instagram and shopping apps, we found it anywhere from 90,000 to 200,000 dinars. [click] That's 122 percent more — for the exact same product.

### Key numbers

| Number | What it means | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 100,000 vs 200,000 IQD | Same model, same Miswag search screen, Oct 20, 2026 | Price evidence screenshots (Sep–Oct 2026) [2] |
| 90,000 – 200,000 IQD | Lowest and highest price found for this model across Instagram, Miswag and iBazzar | Price evidence screenshots (Sep–Oct 2026) [2] |
| +122% | How much more the highest price is than the lowest | Price evidence screenshots (Sep–Oct 2026) [2] |
| ≈ 110,000 IQD | Extra paid at the highest price for the same product | Price evidence screenshots (Sep–Oct 2026) [2] |

### How it's calculated

- Gap: 200,000 − 90,000 = 110,000 IQD.
- Percentage: 110,000 ÷ 90,000 = 1.22, so +122%.

### If they ask

- **Q: Are these really the same product?** A: Yes — every listing on the chart states 25,000 mAh and 165 W (Anker A1695 class). Listings whose model we couldn't confirm were left out.
- **Q: Maybe the 200,000 listing is old or a mistake?** A: It was live in Miswag's search on Oct 20, 2026, right beside the 100,000 one. That's exactly the problem: the shopper can't tell which price is right.
- **Q: Some Instagram posts are older?** A: Yes — they're dated April to October 2026. Even using only same-day app listings, the gap is 100,000 vs 200,000.

## Slide 4 — Solution

### Say

*Time: ~24s*

Today, one product is scattered across pages and apps. Cheapify's rule is simple: [click] one product, one page. [click] Every seller of that product sits on it, showing the doorstep total — item plus delivery. [click] Then our Best Value ranking picks the winner: lowest doorstep total, then fastest delivery, then seller rating. And the ranking can never be bought.

### Key numbers

| Number | What it means | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Doorstep total | Item price + delivery, shown upfront (example: 92,000 + 3,000 = 95,000 IQD — illustration) | Fast Delivery (Duhok) courier quote, Oct 2026 [6] |
| 3,000 IQD | Delivery fee inside Duhok city | Fast Delivery (Duhok) courier quote, Oct 2026 [6] |
| 3 ranking rules | Lowest doorstep total, then fastest delivery, then seller rating | Cheapify go-to-market plan [8] |

### How it's calculated

- Doorstep total = item price + delivery fee.

### If they ask

- **Q: How do you know two listings are the same product?** A: Barcode first. Without a barcode, we match name, specs and photos, and a reviewer approves. Merchants join the existing product page instead of creating a new one.
- **Q: Can a store pay to rank first?** A: No. Ranking is lowest doorstep total, then delivery speed, then seller rating. It is never for sale.

## Slide 5 — Value proposition

### Say

*Time: ~20s*

For shoppers: [click] every price side by side, an order in thirty seconds, and cash on delivery — or card. For merchants: [click] real orders instead of “price?” messages, a free dashboard for stock and payouts, and a fair fee — about 3.8 percent on a typical order.

### Key numbers

| Number | What it means | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ≈ 3.8% | Effective Cheapify fee on a typical 25,000 IQD order (950 IQD) | Cheapify financial model [7] |
| 30 seconds | Checkout design target for a shopper's order | Cheapify go-to-market plan [8] |

### How it's calculated

- Fee on 25,000 IQD: 5% × 10,000 + 3% × 15,000 = 500 + 450 = 950 IQD.
- Effective rate: 950 ÷ 25,000 = 3.8%.

### If they ask

- **Q: Why would merchants pay a fee when Instagram is free?** A: Instagram costs them hours of “price?” messages every day. We send ready orders with address and variant, handle delivery, and charge only on delivered orders — about 3.8% on a 25,000 IQD order.
- **Q: What does “order in 30 seconds” mean?** A: Our checkout design target: pick the seller, confirm the address, choose cash or card.

## Slide 8 — Product

### Say

*Time: ~23s*

Here's how it works. A shopper searches. [click] Sees every seller on one page, Best Value on top. [click] Orders in thirty seconds — cash on delivery, or card. [click] The merchant gets a ready order, packs it, and the courier picks it up. [click] 48 hours after delivery, the merchant is paid automatically, minus our fee.

### Key numbers

| Number | What it means | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 30 seconds | Checkout design target | Cheapify go-to-market plan [8] |
| 48 hours | Return window after delivery; the merchant payout follows when it closes | Cheapify financial model [7] |
| 2,960 IQD | Example fee on a 92,000 IQD item (screen is a concept illustration) | Cheapify financial model [7] |

### How it's calculated

- Example payout: fee on 92,000 IQD = 5% × 10,000 + 3% × 82,000 = 500 + 2,460 = 2,960; 92,000 − 2,960 = 89,040 IQD.

### If they ask

- **Q: Is the app built?** A: It's in development — I'm building it myself. These are concept screens.
- **Q: Why pay merchants after 48 hours, not instantly?** A: That's the return window. Paying once it closes means we never have to take money back.
- **Q: Who handles the cash?** A: Never us. The courier collects it and transfers it to Cheapify electronically; we pay merchants and couriers electronically. Every merchant links a payment account when joining.
- **Q: When do card payments start?** A: Shortly after launch, once our payment-gateway account is approved — that needs the company registration.

## Slide 9 — Business model

### Say

*Time: ~22s*

We only earn when an order is delivered. On a 25,000-dinar order, the shopper pays 28,000. [click] 24,050 goes to the merchant, [click] 2,500 to the courier, [click] 1,450 to us. [click] Our fee gets lighter as prices rise. [click] After about 335 in costs — messages, returns, re-deliveries — we keep 1,115 dinars per order.

### Key numbers

| Number | What it means | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 28,000 IQD | What the shopper pays: 25,000 item + 3,000 delivery | Cheapify financial model [7] |
| 24,050 · 2,500 · 1,450 | Merchant · courier · Cheapify shares of the 28,000 | Cheapify financial model [7] |
| 5% · 3% · 1.5% | Fee tiers: first 10,000 IQD · 10,001–100,000 · above 100,000 | Cheapify financial model [7] |
| 335 IQD | Per-order costs: messages 90, login codes 25, returns 20, re-sent deliveries 200 | Cheapify financial model [7] |
| 1,115 IQD | Profit per delivered order (cash-on-delivery case) | Cheapify financial model [7] |

### How it's calculated

- Fee: 5% × 10,000 + 3% × 15,000 = 950 IQD.
- Merchant: 25,000 − 950 = 24,050.
- Cheapify: 28,000 − 24,050 − 2,500 (courier) = 1,450 = 950 fee + 500 delivery margin (3,000 charged − 2,500 courier).
- Profit: 1,450 − 335 = 1,115 IQD.

### If they ask

- **Q: What is the 335?** A: Per order: WhatsApp messages 90, login codes 25, share of returns 20, re-sending failed deliveries 200.
- **Q: Is the 500 delivery margin real?** A: Fast Delivery quoted 3,000 IQD in the city with a 500 contract discount, so 2,500. We charge 3,000. The contract needs company registration — our first step after funding.
- **Q: And without the contract?** A: Profit drops to about 615 IQD per order and break-even rises to about 1,060 orders a month — slower, but still positive.
- **Q: What about card fees?** A: Not quoted yet; these figures are for cash-on-delivery orders.

## Slide 12 — Financial feasibility

### Say

*Time: ~26s*

We break even at about 585 orders a month — around 20 a day. [click] On our planned pace, the lowest cash point is about 2,200 dollars; we break even in month four and recover everything by month eight. [click] With a strong growth push: month three and month six. [click] Even if growth is slow, we're profitable every month from month seven.

### Key numbers

| Number | What it means | Source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ≈ 585 orders / month | Monthly break-even (about 20 a day) | Cheapify financial model [7] |
| 1,115 IQD | Profit per delivered order | Cheapify financial model [7] |
| −$2,180 (month 3) | Planned case: lowest cash point; break-even month 4; all losses recovered month 8 | Cheapify financial model [7] |
| −$1,800 (month 2) | Fast case: lowest cash point; break-even month 3; recovered month 6 | Cheapify financial model [7] |
| 652,000 IQD | Monthly costs after launch (790,000 from month 6) | Cheapify financial model [7] |

### How it's calculated

- Break-even: 652,000 ÷ 1,115 ≈ 585 orders a month; 585 ÷ 30 ≈ 20 a day.
- Cumulative cash = startup 1.7M IQD + each month's profit minus costs; USD at 1,500 IQD.

### If they ask

- **Q: What are the assumptions?** A: 25,000 IQD average order; costs of 652,000 IQD a month after launch (790,000 from month 6); a signed courier contract; orders growing from 100 to 2,000 a month on the planned pace.
- **Q: What does the slow case look like?** A: Half the planned orders: the lowest cash point is about $2,750 in month 6, we're profitable every month from month 7, and full recovery takes about 20 months. Slower, but the business survives.
